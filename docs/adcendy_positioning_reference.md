# AdCendy — Positioning Reference (one page)

*Internal north-star, not customer copy. This is what our position **is**; the hero messaging brief is how we **say** it. Re-read before a sales call or before writing anything customer-facing — if a decision doesn't fit this page, the decision is wrong, not the page.*

---

## The position

> **AdCendy is the marketing-strategy and market-intelligence unit for companies that can execute but have no one setting direction. You bring the hands; we're the head.**

Everything below is the unpacking of that one line.

---

## Category — what we are, and aren't

A **productized strategy service**. Not an agency (they execute), not a freelancer (a pair of hands for one task), not a SaaS tool (self-serve, no judgment). We lose every comparison made on *someone else's* axis — agencies do more, tools cost less — so we own a different box: evidence-backed strategic direction, delivered as a product.

## Where we sit — upstream, and first

Direction *before* hands. We're the step a company takes to know **what to do**, before they hire freelancers or an agency to **do it**. That makes us complementary to execution providers, not competing with them — "start with AdCendy to know what to do, then bring in them to do it." Position as the first move in the chain, never a rival to the last.

## Who it's for (and who it isn't)

**For:** companies with execution capacity — in-house marketers, freelancers, or an agency on tactics — but **no strategic head** setting direction. Typically a funded early-stage brand or a growing business whose founder has some marketing literacy and hands to deploy, but no coherent strategy tying it together.

**Not for:** buyers who need someone to *execute* (that's an agency); teams that already have a strong in-house strategist (they don't need us, and won't welcome us); solo founders with no execution capacity (a plan needs hands — we're only the head).

---

## What we actually sell (beyond the document)

The artifact is the container. The value is the parts a client's own team structurally *cannot* self-supply:

- **Objectivity** — an outside read free of internal politics and sunk-cost bias. A team can't credibly tell the founder their current approach is wrong; we can.
- **Prioritization under constraint** — for a budget-limited buyer, what to *skip* is worth as much as what to do. We give permission to focus.
- **Speed to direction** — days, not the weeks it would take them to compile and analyze, if they even have someone who can.
- **A coordination spine** — the plan is what the founder, freelancers, and junior marketers all align around. It turns scattered execution capacity into coherent execution.
- **A self-diagnosis framework** — the benchmark ranges ("expect Y in metric Z; on-track is A–B"). A decision tool that outlives the engagement and lets them know if it's working *without us*. Our most differentiated, least obvious deliverable.

When explaining value, foreground **objective direction, focus, and a way to measure whether it's working** — not "you get a document."

## What's in the box

- The competitive and market **intelligence workup** (competitors' advertising/search approach in plain terms, keyword and channel opportunities, market gaps).
- The **strategy** built on it — priorities, positioning, channel and messaging direction, grounded in unit economics.
- The **benchmark framework** — per play: do X → expect Y in metric Z within N weeks → on-track range A–B. *(Ranges must be genuinely sourced and honestly wide — this is our strongest asset only if the numbers are defensible.)*
- A **one-page "what to do first"** action layer on top of the full strategy.
- The **benchmark tracker** as a simple weekly sheet.
- **4 weeks of implementation support** + optional 2 weeks of revision — framed as *helping them diagnose their numbers against the plan*, not as owning their results.

**Refused, on purpose:** execution assets (ad briefs, content calendars, creative), campaign setup, anything done-for-you. The moment the box holds execution, buyers expect execution accountability — which we are not. The box stays strictly direction and measurement.

---

## Price position

Premium versus a template; a fraction of a consultant; 10–50× cheaper than an agency retainer — but for the **direction slice only**. We're the cheapest way to get evidence-backed strategic direction, *not* the cheapest way to get marketing done. Those are different purchases; our price only has to be defensible for the first.

## What we stand for

**Evidence over guesswork, and objective outside direction.** The benchmark framework is the proof of the stance — we don't just assert a plan, we make it testable.

## The line we don't cross

We don't execute, don't manage campaigns, don't replace the team's hands. Say it out loud in the copy — it's the friction reducer that makes the wrong buyers self-select out before they pay.

---

*Defensibility rests on two things we already have: the intelligence is genuinely hard to reproduce (the moat), and we've deliberately refused to compete on execution (where we'd lose). This position is the sum of those two choices. It's complete on paper — the remaining unknown is whether a buyer in the "hands but no head" slice hears it and pays. That's what the outreach tests.*
