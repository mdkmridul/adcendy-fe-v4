# AdCendy — Hero Page Messaging Brief

*Purpose: reposition the hero page from "AI-generated marketing strategy" (which invites the make-vs-buy objection and reads as a commodity PDF) to intelligence-led positioning that pre-qualifies fit. The goal is fewer, better-fit buyers — people self-select out before they pay, so you get less churn and fewer refunds.*

---

## 1. The tone shift (from → to)

The old framing loses because it triggers "why can't my team just do this?" on first read. The new framing answers that objection before it's asked.

| From (avoid) | To (use) |
|---|---|
| "AI-generated marketing strategy" | "Competitive intelligence + a strategy your team can own" |
| "We create your marketing plan" | "We do the market analysis your team can't cheaply reproduce" |
| "Replace expensive consultants" | "The strategic direction your execution is missing" |
| Selling *a document* | Selling *intelligence + direction* (the document is the format, not the pitch) |
| Competing with agencies on price/value | A different category — direction, not execution |

**The single idea the whole page must land:** *we do the hard, hard-to-staff part — decoding your market and competitors — and hand your team a strategy they own and execute. We are not a replacement for their hands; we are the head that points them.*

---

## 2. Voice & tone rules (apply everywhere on the page)

- **Confident, not hypey.** No "revolutionary AI," no "10x your growth." State what you do plainly; specificity reads as competence.
- **Lead with intelligence, not "AI."** The buyer cares that you know what their competitors are doing — not which model wrote it. Mention AI sparingly, as the *how*, never the headline.
- **Never name internal tools or data sources.** Describe capabilities in plain business language ("we analyze your competitors' advertising and search strategy"), never the pipeline behind them. This is a hard rule — tool names undercut confidence and expose the engine.
- **Arm the team, never threaten it.** Every line that could read as "your marketers are redundant" must be rewritten to "your marketers get a power tool." This single choice decides whether teams champion or kill you.
- **Answer the objection on the page, don't hide it.** "Why not have my team do this?" is the first thought every buyer has. Meeting it head-on builds more trust than pretending it doesn't exist.
- **Be honest about what you're not.** The "not for you" section is a feature, not a risk — it's what filters bad-fit buyers before they cost you a refund.

---

## 3. Hero block (top of page)

**Headline (primary):**
> **Intelligence your team can't build. A marketing strategy they can own.**

*Alternates, pick by A/B or gut:*
- The competitive intelligence your team can't build — and the strategy to act on it.
- Know exactly what your competitors are doing. Get the plan to outmanoeuvre them.
- The strategic direction your marketing is missing — grounded in real market intelligence.

**Subhead:**
> AdCendy maps your market — what your competitors are doing across advertising and search, where the keyword and channel opportunities sit, and the gaps no one's filling — then turns it into a clear, executable marketing strategy. You get the direction and the intelligence behind it. Your team runs with it.

**Supporting line (positions against team-threat + agency confusion):**
> Not a marketing agency, and not a replacement for your team — the strategic head that points your execution in the right direction.

**CTAs (lower the first-purchase friction):**
- Primary: **See what's inside a report** → a redacted sample. Let the quality de-risk the price before they commit.
- Secondary: **Check if AdCendy is right for you** → anchors to the fit section below.

*(Avoid "Buy now" as the primary CTA at this price with no proof yet — sample-first converts better cold.)*

---

## 4. On-page section: "Why not just have your team do this?"

Put this high — it's the objection blocking every sale. Answer it directly.

> **Why not just have your team do this?**
>
> Because the hard part isn't writing a plan — it's the intelligence underneath it. Decoding what every competitor in your market is doing across advertising and search, finding the keyword and channel openings, and turning it into direction is days of specialized work per cycle. Most teams have the capacity to *execute* — what they're missing is a dedicated strategist to tell them what to aim at.
>
> AdCendy does the part that's expensive to staff. Your team does what they're good at: making it happen.

---

## 5. What you get / What you don't

This is the honest spec. Publish both columns — the "don't" list is what makes the "get" list credible.

**What AdCendy provides:**
- A full competitive and market intelligence workup — competitor positioning, their advertising and search approach in plain terms, keyword and channel opportunities, and the gaps in your market.
- A marketing strategy built on that intelligence — priorities, positioning, channel and messaging direction, grounded in your unit economics.
- Delivered as a clear document your team owns, executes, and refines — not a black box.
- A strategic starting point at consultant-grade depth, without the consultant timeline or retainer.

**What AdCendy does *not* do:**
- We don't run your marketing. No ad management, no content production, no campaign execution.
- We're not an agency or a done-for-you service.
- We don't replace your marketing team — we give them direction and intelligence to act on.
- We're not useful if you have no way to execute (no team, no freelancers, no capacity). A plan needs hands to run it.

---

## 6. Who it's for / who it's not for (the fit qualifier)

The friction-reducer. Being willing to say "this isn't for you" upfront is exactly what stops the wrong buyers from purchasing, stalling, and refunding. Keep it plain and specific.

**AdCendy is a strong fit if:**
- You have execution capacity — in-house marketers, freelancers, or an agency handling tactics — but no senior strategist or CMO setting direction.
- You're a founder with some marketing literacy who needs a coherent strategy, and a real read on your competition, to point your execution at.
- You're a funded early-stage brand or a growing business that knows *how* to act but needs to know *what* to act on.
- You want to see what competitors are actually doing and where the openings are — without spending weeks compiling it.

**AdCendy is *not* the right fit (yet) if:**
- You need someone to *execute* the marketing, not plan it — you're looking for an agency, and that's a different service.
- You already have a strong in-house strategy team — you likely don't need an outside plan, and your team won't thank you for one.
- You're a solo founder with no execution capacity — a strategy alone won't get you to done. You need hands as well as a head, and we're only the head.

*Optional soft close under this block:* "Not sure which you are? [See a sample report] and decide for yourself."

---

## 7. What this means for your existing comparison table

Your current table compares AdCendy to freelancers and agencies — but on the same axis, that comparison loses, because agencies deliver *more* for their price (they execute). Reframe the table so it's about **category, not competition**:

- Change the question from "who's cheaper/better" to **"what do you actually need right now — direction, or hands?"**
- Columns become: *AdCendy (intelligence + strategy)* · *Freelancer (a pair of hands for one task)* · *Agency (execution + some strategy, on retainer)*.
- Rows should surface the honest trade: AdCendy gives depth of market intelligence and objective direction fast and cheap, but doesn't execute; an agency executes but costs 10–50× and may lack your objective competitive read; a freelancer is cheap and narrow.
- The takeaway row: *"Start with AdCendy when you need to know what to do. Bring in freelancers or an agency to do it."* — this positions you as the **first step**, not a rival, and quietly makes the case that many buyers need you *before* they need them.

---

## Summary — the through-line

Every block above serves one job: make the make-vs-buy answer obvious, and let the buyer decide if they're the "hands but no head" profile *before* they pay. Sell the intelligence (the hard-to-reproduce part), frame the strategy as something the team owns (not a threat), and be openly honest about who shouldn't buy. That honesty is the friction reducer — it costs you a few bad-fit clicks and saves you the refunds and churn that would otherwise land after purchase.
